Brother Beam

We strengthen businesses that already work.

This is the vision, written down so the three of us are building the same company. Everything here is a draft until we say otherwise. The yellow box at the bottom is the list of things only we can decide.

The idea

When a beam in a building is sound but has to carry more than it was built for, a builder does not tear it out. He bolts a new beam alongside it and through-bolts the two together. Builders call it sistering. The old beam keeps doing what it always did. The pair carries the new load.

Most businesses that come to us are that beam. They work. They make money. The owners built them by hand and know every joint. What changed is the load: the market moves differently now, customers expect different things, and the technology that used to be optional is now the floor. The standard advice is to transform, which usually means tearing out what works and hoping the new thing holds.

We do the other thing. We measure the structure as it is actually built, we find what is carrying the weight, and we build the new structure right beside it. AI goes in where the load is, one workflow at a time, bolted to what already works. Nothing that carries the business comes out.

Who it is for

The businesses we want
  • Already successful. Profitable, with real customers and a team. Not a startup, not a turnaround.
  • Owner-led or founder-led. The person who built it is still in the room and can say yes.
  • Facing a market that moved: new technology in the channel, new customer expectations, margin pressure from someone who built it the new way.
  • Big enough that a 10 percent improvement in a core workflow is worth real money.
The businesses we turn down
  • Pre-revenue, or looking for a product to build.
  • Broken businesses that need a rescue, not a reinforcement.
  • Anyone who wants AI for the press release rather than the P&L.
  • Anyone who wants us to run it for them forever. We leave the structure, not a dependency.

What we do

Three practice lines, and each one feeds the next. The names are construction words on purpose, because a client should be able to explain what we did in one sentence.

How an engagement runs

Five moves, every time. The first one is free and short, because it tells both sides whether there is a job here.

0 · Free
Walkthrough

An hour with the owner. We walk the building. We tell them what we would survey and what we would leave alone.

1 · 2 to 4 weeks
Survey

The structure as built, priced. Where value flows, where it leaks, what the market now demands of it.

2 · 1 to 2 weeks
Blueprint

The target structure, the AI and technology that belong in it, the order to build, and the number it should move.

3 · by workflow
Sistering

We build it beside what works, workflow by workflow, with the client’s people trained as we go.

4 · 90 days
Carry

Roadmap, Work Station, weekly cadence, and the before-and-after measured. We stay until the load holds.

Why us

Market first, technology second.

Pat’s whole career is matching how a market actually moves to the technology that fits it. We start from the market and the money, not from a tool somebody wants to sell.

We keep the beam.

Nothing that carries the business comes out. That is a promise the owner can hold us to, and it is the opposite of what most transformation shops do.

We have run the buildings.

We are operators. One of us has run transformation inside large companies through real market cycles. One of us runs a clinic and built its operating system by hand, with AI inside the daily work.

We leave the operating system behind.

Every client gets the same hub we run ourselves: roadmap, Work Station, decisions log, measurement. When we leave, the structure stays and the team knows how to use it.

What only we can decide

Needs a yes from the three of us
  1. The client we mean. What size and what industries first. Mortgage and financial services, where Pat has the network. Consumer products, where he is now. Healthcare, where Mike is. Or the first three that say yes.
  2. The first offer and its price. What a Survey costs, what a Blueprint costs, how Sistering is priced (fixed fee per workflow, or monthly), and what Carry costs for 90 days.
  3. Pat’s time. What he can do while he is at TaylorMade, and when, if ever, that changes.
  4. The third chair. Who the friend is to the company, what they bring, and what that is worth.
  5. Equity and the entity. The split, the entity type, who signs, how money moves.
  6. The domain. brotherbeam.com and brotherbeam.co are open today. Buy when the three of us have said yes to the name.
